The Aspen pipeline grades a frontier model on its ability to author a
hidden test suite for a real broken-access-control and information-
exposure vulnerability — not to fix the bug, but to codify the threat
model that any correct fix would have to satisfy. The agent reads a
small Go MCP service with seeded teams, projects, tasks, comments,
utility behavior, operational surfaces, and sentinel-rich records,
identifies the leak surface across tool responses and serialized
error/result shapes, and writes a test suite that (a) catches ordinary
user overexposure and cross-team data leakage, (b) catches unsafe helper
and operational surfaces that expose seeded internal markers, and
(c) catches over-eager fix shapes that would regress legitimate
same-team participant or admin flows without over-specifying any single
fix shape.

The 16 rubric items decompose the threat model into three structural
groups: legitimate-access and anti-overblock regression guards (5
criteria for same-team participant access, valid tool behavior, admin
cross-team access, second-participant access, and repeatability),
read-side leak and response-shaping coverage (6 criteria across
project-field redaction, comment redaction, search visibility, and
cross-team denial serialization), and helper/operational exposure plus
assertion-quality checks (5 criteria for internal asset fetches,
diagnostic or legacy secret exposure, unsafe formatter behavior,
serialized assertions, and explicit captured values). Severity weights
(critical=4, major=3, minor=2, nitpick=1) reflect the relative impact
of each rubric miss; a perfect suite earns 44/44.
