The Aspen pipeline grades a frontier model on its ability to author a
hidden test suite for a real broken-access-control vulnerability — not
to fix the bug, but to codify the threat model that any correct fix
would have to satisfy. The agent reads a small FastAPI service with a
seeded IDOR-class leak across eight endpoints, identifies the leak
surface, and writes a test suite that (a) catches the leak at every
serialization site, (b) catches over-eager fix shapes that would
regress legitimate participant or admin flows, and (c) doesn't
over-specify any single fix shape.

The 13 rubric items decompose the threat model into three structural
groups: read-side leak coverage (5 criteria across the public read
surface), write-side laundering chains (3 criteria for the
duplicate-then-X workflow shapes), and anti-overblock regression
guards (5 criteria for the dual-contract reasoning that prevents an
over-eager fix from passing). Severity weights (major=3, minor=2,
nitpick=1) reflect the relative impact of each rubric miss; a perfect
suite earns 35/35.
