This Aspen task asks the model to author regression tests for a compact Sanic password-recovery service where `forgot_password()` appends every newly issued secret to `user.reset_tokens` and `reset_password()` accepts any token still present in that list. The bug is therefore a lifecycle failure, not a parsing or routing error: older artifacts are never superseded, winning artifacts are never consumed, and overlapping clients can race multiple valid reset paths against one account. Test authoring is the right evaluation shape because there are several plausible fixes - prune older artifacts on issue, replace the list with one current token, consume on success, or add explicit versioning - but every correct fix must satisfy the same contract: stale and replayed artifacts fail, newest artifacts still work, and the legitimate login or recovery flow shown in `tests/test_smoke.py` survives.

The live rubric has 12 items in three structural groups: 9 critical lifecycle-enforcement checks for latest-only behavior, rejection of older artifacts, replay resistance, cross-client supersession, and concurrent single-winner outcomes; 2 major regression guards for stale-attempt side effects and the post-recovery authentication matrix; and 1 major test-design guard requiring independent persistent clients for multi-request and cross-client flows. With severity weights `critical=4, major=3, minor=2, nitpick=1`, the score ceiling is `9*4 + 3*3 = 45`, which matches `rubric_max_score`.
