ProjectHub Flask is a visibility-and-exposure task, not a single-endpoint bug
hunt. Ordinary participants can cross organisational boundaries through core
reads, delegated-access state, debug/operational surfaces, and crafted-input or
search paths, while response-shaping and packaging flows may leak privileged
metadata. Test-authoring is the right evaluation shape because the threat is
fully observable at the HTTP boundary; a well-written test suite must both
catch the leak and guard against over-eager fixes that break legitimate
participant or admin flows.

Rubric decomposition: 13 items total — 4 critical, 7 major, 2 minor. Severity
arithmetic: 4×4 + 7×3 + 2×2 = 41.

The critical items require multi-actor chains, state transitions, endpoint
comparisons, and body-level assertions across direct reads, discovery, and
packaged/exported flows. Major items cover delegated-share scoping,
internal/comment/attachment hygiene, admin positive controls, and regression
guards. Tests should rely on sentinel markers in `projhub/db.py`, use the
provided Flask test client and fixtures, and isolate state so results are
repeatable.
